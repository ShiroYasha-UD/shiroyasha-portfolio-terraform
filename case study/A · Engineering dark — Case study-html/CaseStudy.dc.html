login & permissions

SSO and permissions, built as a junior

Our platform needed two things: a way to sign in with Google, and a better answer to "who's allowed to do what" than a handful of hard-coded roles. I got to build both.

junior dev on a team of 5 · Aug 2025 – Jan 2026 · Spring Boot, Spring Security, SAML

~150permissions across 9 roles, and still growing
2 → 1ways to log in, Google or password, but only one kind of session afterwards

How it works

Choices I made

Permissions, not just roles

Every endpoint asks for its own permission, and roles like Operator, Driver and Admin are just bundles of them. So a new role means picking permissions, not touching controllers. And if someone needs one extra thing, like creating accounts without being an Admin, they can get exactly that one permission.

A Google user is just a user

Once Google confirms who you are, you become exactly the same kind of user as someone who logged in with a password. One path for everything after login, no special cases.

No surprise accounts

Having a company Google account isn't enough to get in. You have to exist in the system first, so a person still decides who has access.

Looking back

If I did it again, I'd build SSO and RBAC together. Doing them one after the other worked, but it meant wiring login into a permission system that was already finished.
